## B. Attacks

1. **Does the preset hide lambda too well? Verdict: slightly; the larger problem is what “Balanced” claims.** Section 3 exposes the number under Advanced and section 5 repeats it, so a judge can find it, but 0.5 is a coefficient, not evidence of equal outcomes. Keep the presets and display a read-only caption beside them: “Travel-cost weight: 0.50; 0 ignores travel cost, 1 applies the full penalty.” State that Balanced is the middle preset, not a fairness guarantee. Label a slider-adjusted value “Custom (0.xx)” rather than merely clearing the selected name. After each change, announce the updated weighting and affected list. No additional lever is needed.

2. **Dialog versus decide-before-reveal: verdict: the ceremony is broken by disclosure timing, not by modal geometry.** Section 3 exposes town median wait and travel-cost changes before the signature, contradicting PRD 3.1 even if the full metrics panel stays hidden. Before first sign-off, show only ranking changes and the composition of the proposed batch; reveal simulated wait and travel outcomes after the commit. Label those outcomes with comparison baseline, simulated period, assumptions and units; no sign-off is needed to calculate a forecast, but one is required to reveal it under the current rule. Keep a frozen batch, weighting, exception reasons and signer together, with an inspectable order and an audit reference after success. Separately, the fixed design system says “no modal” for sign-off validation and specifies an on-page form: the smallest compliant placement is that same compact review form in a native bordered workspace container. A dialog cannot be called compliant without an explicit rule exception; this review grants none.

3. **Does the visit plan restore the efficiency pull? Verdict: yes, explicitly.** Section 6 optimizes distance and uses signed priority only as a tie-break. A nearby lower-priority stop therefore beats an earlier remote job whenever distance differs; listing a travel saving does not preserve the decision. Make the signed membership and priority the default planning constraint, optimizing only choices that preserve it. If the coordinator requests a departure, show each affected job's old/new order, whether its service day changes, window impact, and travel saving; require a reason and a new signed revision before acceptance. Preserve air/barge jobs as unresolved signed work with a manual next action, rather than treating their absence from the road route as completion. The tenant answer must distinguish formula rank, human override and accepted visit order and use the relevant recorded reason. This repairs the existing plan surface rather than replacing it.

4. **Which section 9 states are missing? Verdict: it is mostly a rendering-state list, not a decision-state contract.** It covers absent selections and service failure but not draft, changed-since-signature, saving, save-failed, signed, superseded or accepted-plan states. Add these transitions to section 9, specifying which snapshot is displayed, what action remains available, and whether the audit write succeeded. Also separate “no open jobs” from “filters exclude jobs,” “no capacity,” “all access blocked” and “all jobs require review”; their recovery actions differ. The individual omissions are listed in D.

## C. Defects

The PRD is binding; section 12 declares some deliberate amendments, which are listed as amendment dependencies rather than reasons to abolish Phase 2. The design system also contains inherited conflicts with the PRD: in particular, it permits displaying unverified values and describes per-job sign-off, while the PRD requires empty ungrounded fields and a signed daily list. Preserve the PRD's product safeguards; do not resurrect those conflicting system examples. No token changes are proposed.

1. **Outcomes revealed before commitment.** Section 3: “Beneath the control, one computed sentence states the effect of the current weighting against efficiency-first: how many remote jobs moved into today's list, the change in town median wait, the change in travel cost.” This breaks PRD 3.1's hidden wait/travel metrics and section 3's own claim that hidden metrics stay hidden. Smallest change: split pre-sign ranking/composition changes from post-sign simulated outcomes, as B2 specifies.

2. **Ungrounded model value leaks into the review.** Section 4: “Model proposed "ventilation" but its phrase was not found in the report.” PRD 3.2 and 5 require the field to remain empty; printing the rejected answer in nearby prose defeats that boundary and anchors the human's choice. Replace it with “Fault type needs review: the proposed source phrase was not found”; keep the rejected value in diagnostic audit data, not the review surface.

3. **Human correction has an incomplete evidence contract.** Section 4: “The coordinator picks the value from the enum, the choice is logged as a human-set field, and the job moves to the ranked list on the next rerun.” PRD 3.1 authorizes human filling, but section 5 still requires source-grounded displayed fields; an enum choice alone does not reconcile those rules. Require the reviewer to select an exact supporting phrase where one exists and record actor and reason. If the report cannot support a required fact, leave it in review with “Request clarification”; any broader human-attestation exception must be explicit in the future PRD amendment, not inferred from this sentence.

4. **Verification is presented as admission to today's crews.** Section 4: “if every required field verified, "Add to today's list"”. The frame gives today's membership to deterministic ranking and human decision, not the extractor. Rename the action “Add to ranked queue”; show its resulting proposed rank and whether it is inside today's proposed batch, without altering an existing signature.

5. **Capacity is asserted too early.** Section 3: “The default tab shows the jobs that fit today's capacity (crews times jobs per crew per day, from `core/constants.py`), ranked.” A slot count does not prove travel, trade or access feasibility, yet the decision strip says jobs “fit” and section 6 later introduces manual exclusions. Replace “fit” with “proposed within job-count capacity” until the stated constraints have been checked, keep counts scoped to the actual crews/regions, and distinguish unplanned signed jobs from feasible crew stops.

6. **Signed order becomes subordinate to distance.** Section 6: “Deterministic: nearest-neighbour from each crew base over haversine distance times the road factor, respecting jobs per crew per day and the signed priority as a tie-break.” This breaks the frame's human-owned order: the default planner changes its organizing rule after signing. Preserve signed priority by default and treat departures as explicit signed revisions (B3).

7. **The tenant cannot explain the actual move.** Section 7: “Content unchanged from PRD §3.4.” Combined with section 6's new reorders, this leaves the formula-based explanation unable to explain the operational decision promised in scenario 3. Append the relevant human move/plan reason and decision version in plain language, distinguishing the signed rank from stop order; retain the exact lambda-zero counterfactual as a formula comparison, not a claimed alternate itinerary.

8. **Audit time is made fictitious.** Section 8: “Timestamps of runtime entries follow the dataset day, not the wall clock, so a demo does not show two calendars.” PRD 3.3 requires a name and timestamp, and the design system's Audit table requires ISO timestamps with a stated local zone and newest-first records. Keep “Decision day (simulation)” separate from actual “Recorded at”; group the override chart by decision day. The two clocks visible in `06-audit_log.png` need clearer labels, not rewritten history.

9. **Provenance is dropped.** Section 2: “Five surfaces replace the six project-shaped pages.” None of their specified headers carries PRD 3's fixed “Geography real (BushTel/ABS); events synthetic” line, although every Phase 1 screenshot does. Add the shared caption to all five surfaces and retain visible sample-data context in intake; do not let a more operational layout imply real work orders.

10. **Rank comparison and row information disappear without replacement.** Section 3: “The one-sentence "why" moves to the details pane; in the row it is a tooltip.” Alongside the single current list, this removes PRD 3.1's side-by-side baseline and readable row explanation; the row specification also omits explicit household health-risk factors and numeric total score, while replacing days-open/days-left with a window fraction. Retain an inspectable baseline comparison with matching columns in the workspace, the numeric score to one decimal, and the missing fields in the selected-job view; keep the explanation persistently readable there, never hover-only. List these exact content changes in the PRD amendment rather than calling all Phase 1 requirements absorbed.

11. **An open ranked job can become impossible to promote.** Section 9: “Job not in today's set” has “Actions disabled with the reason, as in Phase 1.” Phase 1's disabled case in `02-job_card.png` is outside the day's open ranked set; Phase 2 splits that set into today's batch and backlog. Applied literally, the new wording disables promotion of a rankable backlog job across the capacity boundary, undermining PRD 2's human choice. Disable moves only for ineligible/closed/review jobs; allow a backlog promotion with a reason and show which job it displaces.

12. **Fixed sign-off component contract is not preserved.** Section 5: “Sign-off (a dialog from the workspace, not a page)” and “Counts and exceptions, not identifiers.” The system's Sign-off form requires `st.form`, a read-only review summary, named decision controls, inline validation (“no modal”), and success with an audit reference. Use a bordered native form within the workspace, permit opening the exact batch order, and show the audit reference on success. Retain daily-batch semantics from the PRD rather than importing the system's contradictory single-job example; explicitly account for defer/request-information outcomes and decision date.

13. **Colour and display requirements are underspecified where the draft claims compliance.** Section 3: “a stacked score bar in the four factor colours” and “the verbatim report with phrases highlighted in the factor colour”. The system requires non-colour equivalents and a phrase-to-factor legend; the proposed field/value/phrase table does not explicitly map phrases to computed factors, and the row has no numeric segment labels. Add factor names and values in adjacent text, plus the phrase-to-factor legend; use supported native chart/text rendering if the cell bar cannot be implemented. Restore the full label “household health risk” in the sketch rather than “health risk”. Section 3's Advanced slider also needs the system's worded endpoints and current-value/ranking-update caption.

14. **The unchanged tenant screen inherits missing required structure.** Section 7: “Two additions and two fixes” lists only lookup guidance, a policy source, and copy corrections. `04b-tenant-answer.png` is unsectioned text with no next-action or contact block; the fixed Tenant answer rules require question-headed containers, what happens next and who to contact. Explicitly carry those blocks into section 7, using honest unavailable-contact wording rather than inventing a contact or arrival date.

15. **Declared scope amendments are incomplete.** Section 12: “§5: the model also runs at intake time” and “§8: crew visit order moves in as "visit-plan recommendation after sign-off"”. These intentionally contradict PRD 5's build-only model and PRD 2's exclusion of crew routing; changing section 8 alone leaves section 2 contradictory. Add PRD 2 to the amendment list, defining planning as an advisory elaboration of the signed decision and retaining “Nothing is dispatched by the system.” Section 3's Carto basemap similarly requires an explicit amendment to PRD 3.1's offline map guarantee; preserve and specify the local fallback. These dependencies are not implementation permission.

16. **Computed factors are conflated with extracted facts.** Section 3: “Four factor metrics with the phrase that drove each, an em dash and "No source phrase found" where none.” This inherits the design system's Job card wording but conflicts with the frame when applied to logistics and policy/date-derived urgency: their provenance is not necessarily a tenant phrase. Keep missing extracted fields empty, but show valid computed factors with their actual policy, date, geography or access source; never imply that the model extracted a computed score. For genuinely partial computations, apply the system's “Based on N of M fields” caption.

17. **The audit surface loses the record's identity.** Section 8: “Readable column names (kind, day, previous weighting, new weighting, reason, signer, at), no index column, override rate by day on a daily axis, CSV export.” The fixed Audit table rules also require audit reference, job ID, hash, explicit timezone, newest-first append-only rows, filters and export of exactly the filtered rows. Preserve those identifiers and behaviours, using event-specific detail for plan IDs and extraction metadata; renaming columns must not reduce the reasoning record. Keep the override-rate view clearly accessible to the coordinator as PRD 3.6 requires, even when hosted in Evidence lab.

## D. Missing

- Intake: empty/whitespace report, length limit and invalid text submission need validation that preserves entered text.
- Intake: the report's registration ID, reported date, locality and region need a source or explicit input when free text does not provide them.
- Intake: closing the dialog during extraction, reopening it and receiving a late result need defined save/cancel behaviour.
- Intake: double-clicking Extract/Add and retrying after timeout must not create duplicate jobs or audit entries.
- Intake: schema-invalid or partially valid extraction needs field-level failure reasons, with no rejected value displayed.
- Intake: no provider configured, provider unavailable and sample-artefact mode need distinct labels and recovery actions.
- Review: empty queue needs a completion message and a return-to-workspace action.
- Review: next/previous job, multiple missing fields, unsaved edits and leaving the queue need specified behaviour.
- Review: unsupported enum choice, contradictory evidence and no evidence anywhere need “leave pending/request clarification” outcomes.
- Review: successful correction must retain the job ID, announce its new location and provide an action to inspect its proposed rank.
- Workspace: no filter matches, zero crews, blocked access and no open reports must not all say “no open jobs for this day and region”.
- Workspace: filtering must state whether it only changes visibility or also changes the signed batch and available capacity.
- Workspace: distinguish formula-rank delta, manual-move delta and movement into/out of today's batch, including “new to queue”.
- Workspace: changing day, region or weighting after a manual move needs a rule for preserving, invalidating or reapplying that move.
- Workspace: upper/lower move boundaries, direct promotion from backlog, displaced jobs and undo-before-sign need defined controls.
- Selection: a community marker containing several jobs, a region cluster and overlapping points need a job-choice step rather than an arbitrary selected ID.
- Selection: a selected job hidden by a filter, moved between tabs or sent to review needs an explicit retained/cleared-selection rule.
- Selection: sorting the table must preserve job identity; the spike must verify map-to-row navigation and highlighting after reruns.
- Map: loading, missing coordinates, partial tiles and recovery from fallback need states distinct from total basemap failure.
- Evidence: overlapping/repeated source phrases need unambiguous phrase-to-field mapping without altering the verbatim report.
- Retrieval: unavailable index, retrieval failure, outdated/conflicting policy and passage mismatch need different messages from “no match”.
- Metrics: empty town/remote cohorts, incomplete simulation and unavailable travel estimates need em dashes with causes, units and assumptions.
- Sign-off: opening freezes a named batch/version; any changed job, field or weighting before submission must invalidate or refresh that review.
- Sign-off: blank signer/reason, cancel/back, double submission, audit-write failure and successful save need explicit outcomes and focus targets.
- Sign-off: distinguish the selected-for-today IDs from the full ranked backlog in both the review and exported snapshot.
- Sign-off: first signature, post-reveal draft revision and newly signed revision need visible status; logging a slider change alone is not renewed approval.
- Visit plan: no signed road jobs, all jobs manual, closed/unknown road access and missing crew/trade/duration data need honest unresolved states.
- Visit plan: over-capacity, service-window breach, missing travel estimate and infeasible crew allocation must prevent “within capacity” claims.
- Visit plan: pending manual coordination needs an owner/next action and must remain visible after accepting the road portion.
- Visit plan: editing, cancelling edits, requiring an override reason, rejection and regeneration must identify which plan is current.
- Visit plan: a new signed revision must mark an older accepted plan superseded and prevent acceptance of its stale draft.
- Tenant: unknown/malformed ID, review-pending, backlog, unsigned, manually coordinated and superseded-decision answers need distinct copy.
- Tenant: distinguish policy window from a promised visit time and identify the signed decision used by the explanation.
- Audit: no matching rows, failed export, filtered export and partial/missing historical fields need visible outcomes without fabricating values.
- Keyboard: specify list selection → details/evidence → move/reason → review/sign using native focusable controls and a return-to-list action.
- Keyboard: specify dialog entry, Tab/Shift+Tab containment, Escape behaviour and return focus for intake; no draft loss on dismissal.
- Keyboard: define stop-order editing with labelled native controls, retaining focus on the moved job after rerun.
- Accessibility: announce ranking changes, queue removal, validation errors and successful signing; colour or a visual rerender alone is insufficient.
- Accessibility: provide a list-based equivalent for every map action, readable factor values and a narrow-width reading order without custom CSS.
- Feasibility: Task 22 must demonstrate the actual three-way selection, clustered-marker choice, rich row fallback and keyboard path before these are treated as proven.

## E. Verdict

**Reject: signed priority is not authoritative.** Section 6 makes distance the primary ordering rule after the coordinator has signed an equity decision, while section 7 cannot explain that change to the tenant. Repair that decision-to-plan-to-explanation chain and the listed contract/state defects within the proposed workspace; a second design or framework is unnecessary.
