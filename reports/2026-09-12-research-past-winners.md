# Research — What has won the CDU IT Code Fair before, and what the organisers reward

All live-web checks performed **2026-09-12**. Every source is dated. Anything unverified is
marked `TBD — needs validation` rather than guessed.

---

## Claim 1 — The organisers have never published *what* past winners built. Only names.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** The official Past Winners page lists years 2019–2023 as names only —
team members per challenge, sometimes a YouTube link. No project titles, no abstracts, no
datasets, no judge quotes. There is nothing on that page to reverse-engineer a winning
formula from.

- Source: <https://itcodefair.cdu.edu.au/past-winners/> — fetched live 2026-09-12.
- Second, independent source: Wayback capture **2026-04-17** of the same URL
  (`web.archive.org/web/20260417060723/...`) — most recent year present is still 2023.

**What it changes for us:** we cannot copy a past winner. Drop that line of enquiry and
optimise directly against the six published criteria and the brief's own trust twist. Do not
spend more time hunting for exemplars.

## Claim 2 — No 2024 or 2025 winners were ever published, for any challenge.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (absence verified from two dated snapshots).** A 2024 fair was held
(Monday 4 November 2024) and a 2025 fair was held (6 November 2025), but neither year's
results appear on the Past Winners page, the News & Events category, or the Gallery.

- 2024 event held: Radical Systems sponsor post, "2024 Charles Darwin University IT Code
  Fair" — <https://radicalsystems.com.au/news/2024-charles-darwin-university-it-code-fair>
  (fetched 2026-09-12; the post carries no date, no winners, no project detail).
- 2025 event date and awards structure: <https://itcodefair.cdu.edu.au/coding-competition-2/>
  (2025 dates, checked 2026-09-12).
- Absence: past-winners live 2026-09-12 + archived 2026-04-17, both stop at 2023.

**What it changes for us:** there is no public record we will be measured against, and no
public record of *our* result either. Judging is decided in the room on Challenge Day, from
the report and the 10-minute pitch. Budget effort accordingly: the pitch is not a formality.

## Claim 3 — The 2025 AI Challenge was a completely different competition from 2026.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** 2025's AI Challenge was "Empowering Local Languages": build a
PyTorch model translating **Kriol → English**, trained on a supplied dataset, scored on a
*private* test set with the **COMET** metric and a **leaderboard**. Submission was a `.pth`
file plus source; there was a Test Run day (09 Oct 2025) before final submission (15 Oct
2025), and teams ran their model live in front of judges.

- Source: Wayback capture **2025-11-02** of <https://itcodefair.cdu.edu.au/ai-challenge/>
  (`web.archive.org/web/20251102223522/...`), read 2026-09-12.
- Corroborated by still-indexed search descriptions of the same page, checked 2026-09-12.

**What it changes for us:** 2025 was a benchmark shoot-out with one number deciding it. 2026
is the opposite — open "How might we", no metric, six subjective criteria. **Any advice from a
2025 participant about "what wins" is stale and should be ignored.** But one habit carries
over: those judges are used to seeing a model *run live* in the room. Our prototype must run
on Challenge Day, not be screenshotted.

## What past winners had in common

Derived only from what is verifiable — the challenge *topics* the organisers chose, not the
entries, since the entries were never published.

1. **A local or civic problem, never a generic one.** Kriol translation (2025), remote
   connectivity (2026 Data), IT project failure patterns (2023 Data Science), AI-text detection
   (2023 Business). NT relevance is the through-line. Our remote-NT housing brief sits squarely
   on it.
2. **Something that runs in the room.** 2025 required executing the model live before judges;
   2026 gives 10 min pitch + 5 min Q&A face-to-face. A working prototype beats a described one.
3. **Cultural and community framing treated as substance.** The 2025 challenge was explicitly
   framed as "cultural preservation", and 2026 Data asks for "ethical, cultural, and community
   effects". Ethics is a scoring axis here, not a closing paragraph.
4. **Python, reproducible, README with reproduction instructions** — the same submission rule
   across both 2026 challenges and 2025.
5. **Judges are practitioners, not academics** (mostly NTG DCDD). Deployability language wins
   over novelty language.
